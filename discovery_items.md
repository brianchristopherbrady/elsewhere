Easy fix—especially if it only peeled paint and didn’t tear deeply into the drywall paper.

Save the paint chip if you still have it. A paint store can scan it for a close color match.
Remove anything loose. Carefully trim or scrape away paint that’s lifted around the edges. Don’t keep pulling it.
Smooth the edge. Lightly sand with 180–220 grit sandpaper.
Fill the depression. Apply a very thin layer of lightweight spackle with a putty knife or old plastic card. Let it dry, then sand it flush.
Prime it. This is important if you can see brown drywall paper or fuzzy/torn material. Use a shellac- or oil-based stain-blocking primer; water-based primer can make exposed paper bubble. If only the top paint peeled from an intact painted surface, ordinary primer is fine.
Paint the patch. Apply two thin coats using a small roller or dabbing motion rather than a brush stroke. Feather slightly beyond the patch.

If you don’t have matching paint, take the chip to Home Depot, Lowe’s, or a paint store and request a sample-size color match. Matching the sheen—usually flat, eggshell, or satin—is as important as matching the color. Fresh paint may still appear slightly different because the wall has aged; painting from corner to corner is the only way to make a mismatch completely disappear.

For the next Command strip, stretch the removal tab slowly downward, parallel to the wall, rather than pulling the hook outward.

Since the brown drywall paper is exposed, get:

Shellac-based primer — Zinsser B-I-N spray or small can. This seals the paper so it won’t bubble.
Lightweight spackle — a small tub; 3M Patch Plus Primer is fine.
Flexible putty knife — about 2–3 inches wide.
Fine sanding sponge — 180–220 grit.
Matching wall paint — bring the chip for color matching; match the sheen too.
Mini foam roller or small touch-up brush
Painter’s tape
Drop cloth or disposable plastic

Optional but useful:

Utility knife for trimming loose, ragged paint
Tack cloth or microfiber cloth for removing sanding dust

Use them in this order: trim loose edges → seal brown paper with primer → spackle → sand → prime the repair → paint two thin coats. Don’t put water-based spackle directly onto exposed brown paper before sealing it.
